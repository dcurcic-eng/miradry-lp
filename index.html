The problem, named

Antiperspirant treats the surface. The glands keep working.

Grey is out. Light blue is out. There is a spare shirt in the car, a reapplication before the meeting, and a quiet decision to keep your arms down in the photo.

Why it comes back

An antiperspirant plugs the duct at the skin's surface. It washes off, sweats off, and by noon the gland underneath is working exactly as it did before.

Why it smells

Apocrine glands produce a thicker, protein- and lipid-rich sweat. Skin bacteria break it down, and that reaction, osmidrosis, is the odor.

1 in 20 people, over 365 million globally, live with excessive sweating. About 9% of young adults are affected. You are not an outlier.
